The Coastal Town of Essaouira

About two and a half hours from Marrakech, Essaouira trades the desert heat for Atlantic sea breeze. Its whitewashed, blue-shuttered medina is a UNESCO World Heritage site, its port still runs on real fishing boats, and the whole town moves at a noticeably slower pace than Marrakech.

It works well as a full-day trip from Marrakech, or as its own relaxed stop on a longer itinerary — I can arrange either.

What We'll See Together

The Historic Port

A working fishing harbor with blue boats and fresh catch sold right at the docks.

The Medina Ramparts

18th-century sea walls (the Skala) with cannons still lined up facing the Atlantic.

The Beach

A long, wind-swept stretch of sand popular with windsurfers and kitesurfers.

Artisan Woodworkers

Essaouira is known for its thuya-wood marquetry, made by hand in small workshops.

Art Galleries

A town with a real creative streak, full of small independent galleries.

Fresh Seafood

Grilled seafood stalls right by the port, some of the freshest in Morocco.
